Capital account tie-out

Before a new fund administrator goes live, every LP's capital account has to tie to the statement the prior administrator issued. This rolls each account forward from the fund terms and events and names the line that explains every break.

Four tests in all.

Limits

  • One fund, one later close, quarterly events only; no distributions, carried interest, or GP commitment.
  • Later-close LPs bear no share of expenses booked before they joined (LP11 and LP12 carry none of the $60,000 Q1 expenses, though they pay the Q1 catch-up fee). Many fund agreements reallocate those early expenses to later-close investors; that rule would be set from the real LPA.
  • Diagnosis looks for a single missing or double-counted component. Two errors in one account, or a wrong rate rather than a missing line, come back as "review the account by hand".
  • When several components have the same size (LP11), it lists all of them rather than guessing.

Data

All data is made up: a $50M venture fund with 12 LPs, ten admitted at a January 2026 first close and two at a May 2026 later close. It is not based on any real fund, LPA, or administrator's statements, and the terms are simplified (see data/fund_terms.json). A real LPA's fee basis, offset percentage, equalization rate, and allocation rules all have to be confirmed before a tie-out means anything.

Run it yourself

Python 3.9+, standard library only. Everything on this page is the committed output of the code in the repository.

python3 make_example_statements.py
python3 tieout.py
python3 -m unittest discover \
  -s tests -v
